An inverted index is the central data structure of most information retrieval
systems. Instead of mapping documents to the words they contain, it maps each
word (term) to the list of documents that contain it. This list is called a
postings list.

Building an inverted index has two main phases: analysis, where raw text is
tokenized, lowercased and filtered, and indexing, where terms are written to
the postings lists together with term frequencies and positions.

Because the postings lists are sorted by document id, boolean queries can be
answered efficiently by intersecting or merging the lists.
